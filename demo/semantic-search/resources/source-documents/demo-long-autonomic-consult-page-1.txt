REFERRAL QUESTION
Elena was referred after recurrent episodes of dimming vision and leg weakness in upright, warm environments. The consultation asks whether these events represent reflex syncope, orthostatic hypotension, a rhythm disturbance, a vestibular disorder, or another neurologic cause. The records reviewed include an emergency note, orthostatic vital signs, a cardiology report, a head CT report, and a separate vestibular evaluation.

EVENT HISTORY
The earliest event in the available history occurred after several days of reduced fluid intake. Elena rose from a kitchen chair, walked toward the sink, and developed narrowing vision with a sense that she might black out. She held the counter and was helped to a chair. She remained responsive and remembered the event. More recent events have followed prolonged standing while cooking, waiting in a pharmacy line, or talking with neighbors outdoors in warm weather. Sitting or lying down generally improves symptoms within two to five minutes.

Elena uses precise language when comparing symptoms. During upright episodes, the dominant sensation is gray vision, muffled hearing, warmth, and loss of strength rather than rotation of the room. During a different set of brief events triggered by rolling in bed, she experiences spinning without impending loss of consciousness. Those positional spinning events improved after canalith repositioning. The two symptom groups can occur in the same month but have different triggers and associated features.

She denies sudden collapse without warning, exertional chest pain, a sustained racing heartbeat, tongue injury, prolonged confusion, or witnessed tonic-clonic movement. There is no family history of unexplained sudden death. She has never lost bladder control during an event. Her daughter has seen Elena become pale and quiet while standing but has not seen unresponsiveness. Elena has learned to sit at the first sign of visual dimming.

MEDICATION AND INTAKE REVIEW
Current medications include lisinopril, hydrochlorothiazide, metformin, and a nightly statin. She takes the blood-pressure medicines in the morning. On busy days she may drink only two cups of coffee and one glass of water before late afternoon. Breakfast is sometimes skipped. She does not use an alpha blocker, nitrate, tricyclic antidepressant, or sedative. There has been no recent gastrointestinal fluid loss. Home blood-pressure readings are usually in the 120s to 130s systolic while seated.

EXAMINATION
Elena was comfortable and conversant while supine. Cardiac rhythm was regular, lungs were clear, and there was no lower-extremity edema. Speech, eye movements, facial symmetry, strength, sensation, and coordination were normal. Supine blood pressure after ten minutes of rest was 138/74 mmHg with heart rate 72. At one minute standing, blood pressure was 112/68 with heart rate 88. At three minutes standing, blood pressure was 104/66 with heart rate 96. She reported gray vision and heaviness in both legs but no spinning. There was no focal neurologic change.

An abdominal binder was applied and the active stand was repeated after recovery. The systolic pressure decline was smaller, and Elena remained comfortable through five minutes. This response does not establish a single cause by itself, but it supports a clinically meaningful orthostatic component. Carotid sinus massage was not performed. The prior ambulatory rhythm report did not identify sustained tachyarrhythmia or a clinically significant pause during patient-triggered recordings.
